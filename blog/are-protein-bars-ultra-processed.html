Are Protein Bars Ultra-Processed? Quest, RXBAR & Grenade

Protein bars are sold as gym fuel and healthy snacks, but most protein bars are ultra-processed. Not all of them, though, and the brands with the shortest labels aren't always the ones you'd expect. We compared the ingredients of seven real bars from the UK, US and Australia to show where each one lands.

The quick answer

Most protein bars are ultra-processed (NOVA Group 4). The usual reasons are protein isolates, sugar alcohols such as maltitol or erythritol, non-sugar sweeteners like sucralose, and flavourings. A bar made only from whole foods, such as dates and nuts, is not ultra-processed. In between are "clean label" bars like RXBAR and Nakd, where natural flavouring is the only marker. The grams of protein on the front tell you nothing about processing. The ingredient list does.

What makes a protein bar ultra-processed?

NOVA classifies food by how it is processed and why, not by protein, sugar or calories. A bar becomes ultra-processed when it is built from industrial ingredients you wouldn't find in a kitchen, or contains additives whose job is to make it taste, feel or look like something it isn't.

Protein bars hit several of these markers at once. That's because the product itself is a hard engineering problem. The aim is to pack 20g or more of protein into a chewy, sweet bar with little sugar that stays soft on a shelf for months. Whole foods can't easily do that, so manufacturers use:

  • Protein isolates and concentrates (milk protein isolate, whey protein isolate, calcium caseinate, soy protein isolate, hydrolysed collagen). These are proteins extracted from their original food. NOVA lists them as substances found only in ultra-processed foods.1
  • Sugar alcohols (polyols) such as maltitol, xylitol and erythritol, used to replace sugar's sweetness and bulk.
  • Non-sugar sweeteners such as sucralose and steviol glycosides.
  • Bulking agents and humectants such as polydextrose and glycerol, which add fibre grams and keep the bar soft.
  • Emulsifiers and flavourings that make a bar taste like cookie dough or caramel.

Group 1 · Minimally processed

Nuts, seeds, dried fruit and cocoa, pressed or mixed together with nothing added.

Group 3 · Processed

Whole foods combined with culinary ingredients such as honey, sugar, oil or salt, for example a simple homemade flapjack of oats, butter and honey.

Group 4 · Ultra-processed

Any recipe that includes protein isolates, polyols, non-sugar sweeteners, emulsifiers or flavourings. This covers most bars sold as "protein bars".

US: Quest, RXBAR and Larabar

Quest Protein Bar, Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough

Ingredients: Protein blend (milk protein isolate, whey protein isolate), polydextrose (prebiotic fiber), almonds, water, unsweetened chocolate, erythritol, cocoa butter, glycerin; less than 2% of natural flavors, salt, lecithin, stevia sweetener and sucralose.

Our assessment: Group 4. This is a textbook formulation. Two protein isolates provide the protein, polydextrose adds bulk and fibre, erythritol, stevia and sucralose replace sugar, and natural flavors supply the cookie-dough taste. The almonds and chocolate are real foods, but they aren't what the bar is built on.

RXBAR Chocolate Sea Salt

Ingredients: Dates, egg whites, cashews, almonds, chocolate, cocoa, natural flavors, sea salt.

Our assessment: Group 4, borderline. Every ingredient except one is a whole food. The protein comes from egg whites, not an isolate, and the dates do the binding and sweetening. Natural flavors are the only marker. On a strict reading of NOVA that makes it Group 4, but this bar is much closer to food than a Quest or Grenade bar.

Larabar Cashew Cookie

Ingredients: Cashews, dates.

Our assessment: Group 1. Two whole foods pressed together, with no added sugar, oil, salt or flavouring. The trade-off is that this is a fruit-and-nut bar, not a high-protein bar. It has about 5g of protein, against 10g to 45g for the protein bars here.

UK: Grenade and Nakd

Grenade OREO Protein Bar

Ingredients: Protein blend (calcium caseinate, whey protein isolate), milk chocolate with sweetener (20%) (maltitol, cocoa butter, whole milk powder, cocoa mass, soy lecithin, natural flavouring), bovine collagen hydrolysate, glycerol, maltitol, sucralose, palm oil, water, fat-reduced cocoa powder (3%), wheat flour, wheat starch, rapeseed oil, polydextrose, sea salt, soy lecithin, raising agents (ammonium carbonates, sodium carbonates), acidity regulator (sodium hydroxide), flavouring.

Our assessment: Group 4. Two extracted milk proteins and a collagen hydrolysate supply the 21g of protein. Maltitol sweetens both the coating and the bar, alongside sucralose, polydextrose, emulsifiers and flavourings. Each 60g bar holds 17g of polyols, so the label carries the legally required warning that excessive consumption may cause a laxative effect. That warning is a quick sign of a heavily reformulated bar.

Nakd Peanut Delight

Ingredients: Dates (53%), peanuts (46%), sea salt, natural flavouring.

Our assessment: Group 4, borderline. Like RXBAR, this is 99% whole food, and the natural flavouring is the only marker. Without it, dates, peanuts and salt would fit Group 3. Nakd bars are fruit-and-nut bars rather than protein bars, but they sit on the same shelf and are often bought for the same reason.

Australia: Carman's and Musashi

Carman's Dark Choc & Cranberry Protein Bar

Ingredients: Nuts 26% (peanuts, almonds), plant protein blend (soy protein crisps [isolated soy protein, tapioca starch, salt], soy protein isolate), glucose, seeds 11% (sunflower, pepitas, sesame), honey, dark choc compound 9% (sugar, vegetable oil, cocoa powder, emulsifiers [soy lecithin, sunflower lecithin], salt, natural flavour, milk), cranberries 7% (sugar, cranberries, sunflower oil), sunflower oil, natural flavours, cinnamon.

Our assessment: Group 4. This bar looks like whole food: you can see the nuts, seeds and fruit. But the protein boost comes from soy protein isolate and crisps made from it, and it also contains glucose, compound chocolate and natural flavours. Most of the bar is real food, but it still fits Group 4, and that is typical of "natural" protein bars.

Musashi High Protein Bar (90g), Peanut Butter

Ingredients: Protein blend (50%) (hydrolysed collagen, calcium caseinate, soy protein isolate, whey protein isolate, emulsifiers [soy lecithin, 471]), protein milk chocolate (18%) (cocoa butter, erythritol, steviol glycosides, milk solids, soy protein isolate, cocoa mass, inulin, soy lecithin, flavour), glycerol, water, roasted peanuts (4%), peanut flour (2%), flours, salt, soy lecithin, sucralose.

Our assessment: Group 4. This is the extreme end of the shelf. At 45g of protein per bar, half the bar is a blend of four extracted proteins. Peanuts make up just 4%, even though the bar is sold as peanut butter flavour.

Are "clean label" bars like RXBAR and Nakd ultra-processed?

Strictly, yes. In practice, they are much closer to whole food than typical protein bars. NOVA treats flavourings, including "natural" ones, as a cosmetic additive characteristic of ultra-processed foods. That is why a single line of natural flavouring moves RXBAR and Nakd into Group 4 on paper.

It is worth being honest about how small that difference is. A bar made of dates, egg whites and nuts with a dash of flavouring is a very different food from one that is half protein isolate and sweetened with three different polyols. NOVA is a classification, not a sliding scale, so it can't show that gap. If you're comparing bars, compare how much of each label is real food, and look at what the protein is made of.

We applied the same rule in our Greek yogurt comparison: a fruit yogurt with natural flavourings is Group 4 even when the rest of the pot is strained yogurt and strawberries.

More protein doesn't make a bar healthier

The more protein a bar has, the more likely it is to be ultra-processed. Whole foods dilute protein with fat, fibre and carbohydrate: nuts are roughly 20% protein and dates almost none. To reach 20g, 30g or 45g in a single bar, manufacturers need concentrated protein from isolates. The protein number on the front is often a direct measure of how much isolate is inside.

That doesn't make the protein bad. Whey and milk proteins are high-quality proteins. The concern with ultra-processed foods is the whole formulation and the eating patterns it encourages. In a controlled trial at the US National Institutes of Health, people ate about 500 more calories a day on an ultra-processed diet than on an unprocessed one, even when meals were matched for protein, sugar, fat and fibre.2 Tufts University nutrition experts made a similar point about bars this year: some are little more than candy bars, with as much sugar and as many calories.3 We cover the weight evidence in more detail in Do Ultra-Processed Foods Make You Gain Weight?

If you mainly want more protein, whole-food options get you there without the isolates. Plain Greek yogurt (often around 10g per 100g), eggs, a tin of fish, edamame, or a handful of nuts with a piece of cheese all work. A plain peanut butter made only from peanuts and salt on wholegrain toast is a good post-workout option too.

A quick protein bar shopping checklist

  1. Read the first three ingredients. If you see "protein blend", any isolate, "calcium caseinate" or "hydrolysed collagen", the bar is Group 4.
  2. Scan for polyols and sweeteners. Maltitol, xylitol, erythritol, sorbitol, sucralose, acesulfame K and steviol glycosides are all Group 4 markers.
  3. Check for the laxative warning. In the UK and EU, it's required on foods with more than 10% added polyols. If it's there, the bar has been heavily reformulated.
  4. Don't stop at "natural". Natural flavourings still count as a marker. It's the only one in RXBAR and Nakd.
  5. For a non-UPF bar, look for a list you could make at home: nuts, dates, seeds, cocoa, maybe salt. Expect less protein in exchange.

Frequently asked questions

Are all protein bars ultra-processed?

No, but most are. Any bar that gets its protein from isolates or concentrates (whey, milk, soy, pea or collagen) is Group 4. Bars made only from whole foods, such as Larabar Cashew Cookie (cashews and dates), are not ultra-processed. They usually have much less protein.

Is Quest Bar ultra-processed?

Yes. The Quest Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough bar we checked is built on milk and whey protein isolates, with polydextrose, erythritol, stevia, sucralose and natural flavors, so it fits NOVA Group 4.

Is RXBAR ultra-processed?

Only just. RXBAR Chocolate Sea Salt is made of dates, egg whites, nuts, chocolate, cocoa and sea salt. The one Group 4 marker is natural flavors. Strictly that makes it ultra-processed, but it is much closer to whole food than most protein bars.

Are Grenade bars ultra-processed?

Yes. The Grenade OREO Protein Bar we checked contains calcium caseinate, whey protein isolate, collagen hydrolysate, maltitol, sucralose, polydextrose, emulsifiers and flavourings. Its 17g of polyols per bar is why the label carries a laxative warning.

Are protein bars bad for you?

Not necessarily as an occasional snack, but they're often less healthy than they look. NOVA describes processing, not nutrition, so also compare sugar, saturated fat and fibre. Research links diets high in ultra-processed foods with eating more calories and with higher risk of several chronic diseases.2 If you eat a protein bar every day, it is worth checking whether a whole-food snack could do the same job.

What is the least processed protein bar?

Look for a bar whose label lists only whole foods: nuts, seeds, dates or other dried fruit, cocoa and perhaps salt. Of the bars we checked, Larabar Cashew Cookie is the only one with no Group 4 markers. RXBAR and Nakd come next, with natural flavouring as their only marker.
