Is Granola Ultra-Processed? In the 7 We Checked, Often One Ingredient Decides

Granola sits in the "healthy" part of the cereal aisle, but is granola ultra-processed? Some is and some isn't, and the difference is often a single line near the end of the ingredient list. We compared seven real granolas and granola bars from the US, UK and Australia to show where each one lands.

The quick answer

Granola is not automatically ultra-processed. Oats, nuts, seeds, oil and a sweetener such as honey or sugar, baked together, make a processed food (NOVA Group 3). A granola becomes ultra-processed (Group 4) when its recipe adds industrial ingredients such as flavourings, emulsifiers, glucose or tapioca syrup, modified starch or added fibres. Granola bars are more often Group 4 than loose granola, because they need binders to hold their shape.

Two of the seven products we checked are not ultra-processed. Two clearly are. The other three are Group 4 because of one ingredient each:

What makes granola ultra-processed?

NOVA classifies food by how it is processed and why, not by sugar, fibre or calories. Granola is made by mixing rolled oats with oil and a sweetener, then baking it until it clusters and crisps. Rolling oats and baking are ordinary food processes, the same kind you could do at home. They don't make granola ultra-processed.

What can is a recipe that also uses ingredients found mainly in industrial food manufacturing. NOVA's authors list sugars and starches made by further processing (such as maltodextrin, invert sugar and high-fructose corn syrup) and cosmetic additives such as flavourings, emulsifiers and thickeners as markers of ultra-processing.1 Their list of typical ultra-processed foods also names breakfast cereals and cereal bars, which is why the recipe matters more than the category.

Group 1 · Minimally processed

Plain rolled oats, unsweetened untoasted muesli made only of oats, nuts, seeds and dried fruit.

Group 3 · Processed

Oats, nuts and seeds baked with oil and a culinary sweetener such as sugar, honey, maple syrup or golden syrup. Most homemade granola lands here.

Group 4 · Ultra-processed

A granola or bar that adds markers such as flavourings, emulsifiers, glucose or tapioca syrup, modified starch, added fibres like chicory root fibre, or humectants like glycerine.

Granola is baked, not extruded. Unlike puffed or shaped cereals, which are pushed through industrial extruders, granola is still recognisably oats. That's why it can stay in Group 3 when the recipe is simple.

US: Purely Elizabeth, KIND and Nature Valley

Purely Elizabeth Original Ancient Grain Granola

Ingredients: Organic Certified Gluten-Free Oats, Organic Coconut Sugar, Organic Coconut Oil, Organic Sunflower Seeds, Organic Puffed Amaranth, Organic Quinoa Flakes, Organic Chia Seeds, Organic Cinnamon, Sea Salt.

Our assessment: Group 3. Every ingredient is a grain, seed, oil, sugar, spice or salt you could buy and use at home. Coconut sugar is still sugar, so this isn't a low-sugar food, but nothing on the label is a Group 4 marker.

KIND Oats & Honey Granola with Toasted Coconut

Ingredients: Oats, Tapioca Syrup, Brown Rice, Cane Sugar, Honey, Millet, Oat Flour, Coconut, Canola Oil, Buckwheat, Amaranth, Molasses, Quinoa, Brown Rice Syrup, Sea Salt, Vitamin E (Tocopherols to Maintain Freshness).

Our assessment: Group 4, borderline. Five whole grains, honey and coconut make up most of this, and vitamin E here is just an antioxidant to stop the oil going rancid. The marker is tapioca syrup, the second ingredient. It is made by breaking down tapioca starch into glucose with enzymes, the same process used for glucose syrup, so it's an industrial sugar rather than a kitchen one. It's also there in large amounts, as the binder that makes the clusters.

Nature Valley Oats 'n Honey Crunchy Granola Bars

Ingredients: Whole Grain Oats, Sugar, Canola and/or Sunflower Oil, Rice Flour, Honey, Salt, Brown Sugar Syrup, Baking Soda, Soy Lecithin, Natural Flavor.

Our assessment: Group 4. This is a short list, and most of it is what you would use to make granola at home. Soy lecithin (an emulsifier) and natural flavor are the two markers. It's a good example of a product that's ultra-processed by definition but much simpler than most bars in the snack aisle.

Note that the Canadian Nature Valley Oats 'n Honey bar has a different recipe, with monoglycerides instead of natural flavor. Check the label for the country you're in.

UK: Jordans and Dorset Cereals

Jordans Crunchy Oat Granola Raisin & Almond

Ingredients: Wholegrain Oat Flakes (68%), Sugar, Raisins (7%), Sunflower Oil, Sultanas, Whole & Sliced Almonds (3.5%), Honey (1%), Sunflower Seeds, Natural Flavouring.

Our assessment: Group 4, borderline. Two-thirds of the pack is oats, and everything but the last ingredient is a whole food or a kitchen ingredient. Natural flavouring is the only marker. Without it, this would be Group 3.

Dorset Cereals Honey Granola

Ingredients: Wholegrain Oat flakes (46%), nuts & seeds (28%) [sliced Almonds, sunflower seeds, pumpkin seeds, chopped Pecan Nuts], honey (16%), toasted wholegrain Rye flakes, vegetable oil (rapeseed and sunflower in varying proportions), natural flavouring.

Our assessment: Group 4, borderline. Like Jordans, this is oats, nuts, seeds, honey and oil, with natural flavouring at the end. Dorset's Nutty Granola, which uses golden syrup instead of honey, also ends with natural flavouring.

Both of the UK's best-known granola brands make granola that's almost Group 3. If avoiding Group 4 matters to you, look for a granola whose list ends with an ingredient you recognise, or make your own.

Australia: Carman's and Uncle Tobys

Carman's Original Fruit Free Muesli

Ingredients: Whole Grain Oats, Nuts 10% (Almonds, Pecans), Golden Syrup, Seeds 8% (Sunflower, Pepitas, Sesame), Sunflower Oil, Cinnamon, Vitamin (Vitamin E).

Our assessment: Group 3. Carman's calls this a toasted muesli, but it's made the way granola is: oats, nuts and seeds baked with oil and syrup. Golden syrup is a traditional cane sugar syrup and counts as a culinary sweetener (see syrups and sugars below). Vitamin E protects the oil. There's no flavouring, and that's the only real difference between this and the two UK granolas.

Uncle Tobys Chewy Choc Chip Muesli Bar

Ingredients: Muesli blend [Uncle Tobys Rolled Oats (34%), wheat (11%), breakfast cereal (whole grain wheat (5%), wholemeal wheat flour, wheat bran, sugar, vitamin E), coconut, milk powder], glucose, choc chips (9%) [sugar, vegetable oil, milk solids, cocoa, cocoa mass, emulsifiers (492, soy lecithin), salt], chicory root fibre, sunflower oil, sugar, humectant (glycerine), modified starch (1412), invert sugar, honey, emulsifier (soy lecithin), flavour, salt.

Our assessment: Group 4. This is the most heavily formulated product we checked. It has glucose and invert sugar, added chicory root fibre, glycerine to keep it chewy, a modified starch, two emulsifiers and a flavour. "50% wholegrain" and "no artificial colours or flavours" are true claims on the pack, but neither answers the NOVA question.

Carman's and Uncle Tobys both put oats first and both sell muesli bars, but they sit at opposite ends of the scale. "Muesli" on the front doesn't tell you which kind you've got.

Honey, golden syrup, tapioca syrup: which sweeteners count?

Every granola needs something sweet and sticky to make clusters, so the sweetener is the most important line on the label. NOVA draws the line between sweeteners you could buy for your kitchen and ones made by further industrial processing of starch or sugar.

  • Culinary sweeteners (compatible with Group 3): sugar, cane or coconut sugar, honey, maple syrup, molasses and golden syrup. These are extracted from a plant or made by bees and have long been kitchen staples.
  • Industrial sweeteners (Group 4 markers): glucose and glucose syrup, tapioca syrup, invert sugar, dextrose, maltodextrin, fructose and high-fructose corn syrup. These are made by breaking down starch or splitting sugar in a factory.

There are grey areas. Golden syrup is partly inverted during refining, and brown rice syrup is made by breaking down rice starch with enzymes, much like tapioca syrup. We treat golden syrup as a culinary ingredient because it has been a home baking staple for well over a century. We count tapioca syrup in KIND as a marker because it's the main binder, not a minor ingredient. Other classifiers may draw these lines slightly differently.

The amount of sugar is a separate question. A Group 3 granola can still be high in sugar. Purely Elizabeth and Carman's aren't ultra-processed, but they are sweetened. Read the nutrition panel as well as the ingredients.

Are granola bars ultra-processed?

Usually, yes. Loose granola only has to hold together in clusters. A bar has to stay in one piece in a wrapper for months without going hard or crumbling. That job usually needs binders such as glucose syrup, humectants such as glycerine, emulsifiers, and sometimes added fibre to raise the fibre claim.

Both bars in our sample are Group 4. Nature Valley's crunchy bar gets there with just an emulsifier and a flavouring, while Uncle Tobys' chewy bar has seven different markers. Chewy bars tend to need more additives than crunchy ones, because staying soft is harder than staying crisp.

Not every bar is ultra-processed. Bars made only from dates, nuts and oats exist, and Carman's sells muesli bars too, though we didn't check those here. Our protein bars comparison found one bar made only of whole foods among seven. The same label check works for granola bars.

A quick granola shopping checklist

  1. Read to the end of the list. In three of our seven products the only Group 4 marker was the last or one of the last ingredients.
  2. Check the sweetener. Sugar, honey, maple or golden syrup are fine for Group 3. Glucose, tapioca syrup, invert sugar or dextrose push it into Group 4.
  3. Look for "flavouring" or "natural flavor". It's the most common marker in otherwise simple granolas.
  4. Be more suspicious of bars than loose granola. Emulsifiers, glycerine, added fibre and modified starch are common in bars and rare in loose granola.
  5. Ignore the front of the pack. "Wholegrain", "no artificial flavours", "high fibre" and "natural" don't describe processing.
  6. Or make it. Oats, nuts, seeds, a little oil and honey or maple syrup, baked at about 160°C (320°F) for 20–25 minutes, gives you a Group 3 granola with no labels to read.

Frequently asked questions

Is granola a processed food?

Yes. Even the simplest granola is oats baked with oil and a sweetener, which makes it a processed food (NOVA Group 3). "Processed" and "ultra-processed" aren't the same thing. Granola is ultra-processed only when the recipe adds industrial ingredients like flavourings, emulsifiers or glucose syrup.

Is homemade granola ultra-processed?

No. Granola made at home from oats, nuts, seeds, oil and honey, sugar or maple syrup is Group 3, because every ingredient is a food or a kitchen ingredient.

Is Nature Valley granola ultra-processed?

The Nature Valley Oats 'n Honey Crunchy Granola Bars we checked are Group 4, because they contain soy lecithin and natural flavor. The rest of the list is oats, sugar, oil, rice flour, honey, salt, brown sugar syrup and baking soda, so it's a simpler recipe than most granola bars.

Is muesli less processed than granola?

Untoasted muesli made only of oats, nuts, seeds and dried fruit is usually Group 1, because nothing is added and nothing is baked in. Toasted muesli, like Carman's, is closer to granola and usually Group 3 or 4 depending on the recipe. Some dried fruit is coated in oil or sugar, so check that too.

Are corn flakes ultra-processed?

Most boxed breakfast cereals are classed as ultra-processed, and NOVA's own examples include breakfast cereals. We covered Corn Flakes and other common cereals in our breakfast foods roundup. Plain granola is often less processed than flaked or puffed cereals, but it still depends on the recipe.

Is granola healthy?

It can be, but NOVA doesn't answer that question on its own. A Group 3 granola can still be high in sugar and calories, and a small serving goes a long way. Research links diets high in ultra-processed foods with eating more calories,2 so choosing a Group 3 granola and watching the portion is a reasonable approach.
